# Vision

## Purpose

Give Jeff Whiteside a credible, self-owned professional presence at `jeffwhiteside.dev` that
supports a senior engineering leadership job search — Director, Senior Director, or VP of
Engineering.

The site answers one question quickly: *what kind of engineering leader is this, and is he a
fit for the role we are hiring?*

## Intended audience

In priority order:

1. **Hiring managers and executives** (CTO, CPO, VP Engineering, founders) evaluating fit for
   a senior leadership role.
2. **Recruiters** — internal and agency — scanning for relevant scope and experience.
3. **Peers and colleagues** following up after a conversation, referral, or LinkedIn contact.

All three arrive with limited time. The first two typically scan before they read.

## Primary positioning

A software engineering leader with more than 20 years of experience spanning hands-on
development, engineering leadership, SaaS platforms, product partnership, and organizational
scaling.

The site emphasizes:

- SaaS engineering leadership across multiple teams and products
- Manager-of-managers leadership and developing engineering leaders
- Platform modernization and delivery-system improvement
- Reliability and release discipline — CI/CD, observability, incident response, RCA, SLO/SLA
- Engineering dashboards and operating metrics
- Marketplace and transaction-heavy systems
- Practical, applied use of AI in engineering and in product
- Genuine, current technical capability

It deliberately does **not** claim depth in: infrastructure engineering, DevOps as a primary
discipline, data science leadership, ML research, cybersecurity leadership, full billing
platform ownership, usage-based billing, or subscription-management platforms.

## Design principles

1. **Credible over impressive.** No claim appears that could not be defended in an interview.
2. **Scannable first, readable second.** Structure and hierarchy carry the message before the
   prose does.
3. **Restraint as a signal.** Calm typography and generous whitespace communicate seniority
   more effectively than visual effects.
4. **The artifact is evidence.** The site itself should demonstrate engineering judgment —
   proportional architecture, real documentation, accessible markup.
5. **Proportional engineering.** A personal site does not warrant a CMS, a database, or a test
   pyramid. Complexity must earn its place.
6. **Accessible by default.** Semantic HTML, AA contrast, visible focus states, keyboard
   navigation.
7. **Honest placeholders.** Incomplete content is labeled as incomplete rather than invented.

## Initial scope (Version 1)

A single page with:

1. Header and navigation
2. Hero
3. Leadership focus
4. Selected experience
5. Projects
6. Writing and publication
7. Contact
8. Footer

Plus: responsive layout, a polished light theme, and appropriate metadata.

## Non-goals

Explicitly out of scope for Version 1:

- Dark mode
- A blog or CMS
- A database or authentication
- A contact-form backend
- Multiple pages or project case-study pages
- An AI assistant or any AI integration
- A downloadable résumé
- Interactive architecture diagrams on the public site
- Animation libraries or component libraries
- Automated tests beyond what the implementation warrants

These are recorded in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md) as deferred, not quietly prepared for.

## Measures of success

The site is successful if:

- A hiring manager understands the scope and level of leadership within roughly 30 seconds.
- Nothing on the page requires walking back or qualifying in an interview.
- It reads as senior — not as a portfolio, a résumé template, or a generated landing page.
- It loads fast, works well on a phone, and is fully keyboard accessible.
- It is easy to update as the search progresses.
